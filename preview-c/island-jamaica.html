Jamaica

Parish registers from 1664, civil registration from 1878, and our largest sub-group to help you read them.

Most of our members have a Jamaican line, so this is where the group has the most experience. The records are good, but they are spread across several archives and websites, and it helps to know which to try first.

Where the records are

  • Anglican parish registers of baptisms, marriages and burials survive for some parishes from 1664. Copies are held by the Registrar General’s Department in Spanish Town, and many were filmed by FamilySearch.
  • Civil registration of births and deaths began in 1878 and of marriages in 1880.
  • The slave registers for Jamaica, taken every three years from 1817 to 1832, are at The National Archives in Kew and online through Ancestry.
  • The Jamaica Archives and Records Department in Spanish Town holds wills, court records and many church records.

How the group can help

Bring what you have to a monthly meeting. Members can show you how to search the FamilySearch collections free of charge at any Solihull library, and how to read the older registers. We have also had talks on Jewish ancestry in Jamaica.

The Jamaica sub-group

The Jamaica sub-group meets between the main meetings to work through research in more depth. Dates are posted in What’s on.
