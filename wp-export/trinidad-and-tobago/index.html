Trinidad and Tobago

Trinidad’s records reflect everyone who came: Spanish and French settlers, enslaved Africans, free Black American soldiers and, from 1845, indentured workers from India. Much of it is still on paper in Port of Spain, not online, so it helps to know what to ask for. Tobago was a separate colony until 1889 and has records of its own.

The main record sources

  • National Archives of Trinidad and Tobago

    In Port of Spain. Holds the slave registers, newspapers, an 1851 census, land tax assessments for 1847, 1851 and 1852, and the registers of Indian immigrants from 1845 to 1917.

    Visit the National Archives

  • Registrar General’s Department

    Births, deaths and marriages. You apply on a form. It helps to have the mother’s name for a birth, and the district or hospital for a death.

  • The churches

    Roman Catholic registers matter more here than on most islands, because of Trinidad’s Spanish and French past. Ask the parish church for a baptism: the baby’s name will be on the record.

  • FamilySearch

    Free. Trinidad and Tobago has far fewer records online than Jamaica or Barbados, so check what is listed before you plan a search.

    Search Trinidad and Tobago on FamilySearch

  • Slave registers and compensation records

    Registers of enslaved people made from 1813 in Trinidad, the first in the Caribbean, and from 1819 in Tobago, up to 1834, at The National Archives (series T 71), and searchable on Ancestry. The Legacies of British Slavery database at UCL covers the owners and estates paid compensation.

    Search Legacies of British Slavery

  • At Solihull Core Library

    Solihull Libraries list the Caribbean family history films they hold, and you can use FamilySearch and Ancestry there for free. Ask us at a meeting and we’ll show you.

    See the library's list of films
