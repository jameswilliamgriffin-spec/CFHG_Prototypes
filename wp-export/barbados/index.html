Barbados

Barbados has some of the oldest registers in the Caribbean: Church of England baptisms, marriages and burials go back to 1637 in some parishes. The eleven parishes have hardly changed since the 1640s, so the parish in an old record is the one on the map today. Our Barbados research group, for members, meets on Zoom each month.

The main record sources

  • FamilySearch

    Free. Church of England records from 1637 to 1887, and parish register transcripts for 1900 to 1931. Our Barbados group found some of these locked and asked FamilySearch to open them: they can now be viewed anywhere in the world.

    Search Barbados on FamilySearch

  • Barbados Department of Archives

    At Black Rock, St Michael. Holds the original parish registers, wills from the 1640s, deeds and plantation records.

  • Registration Department

    The state registered births from 1890 and deaths from 1925. Order certificates from the Registration Department in Bridgetown.

  • Slave registers and compensation records

    Registers of enslaved people made between 1817 and 1834, at The National Archives (series T 71), and searchable on Ancestry. The Legacies of British Slavery database at UCL covers the owners and estates paid compensation.

    Search Legacies of British Slavery

  • Findmypast

    Barbados baptisms from 1637 to 1891 and marriages from 1637 to 1887, indexed by name. A subscription site, free to use in many libraries.

    Visit Findmypast

  • At Solihull Core Library

    Solihull Libraries list the Caribbean family history films they hold, and you can use FamilySearch there at affiliate level. Ancestry is free to use in the library and includes English settlers in Barbados from 1637 to 1800.

    See the library's list of films
