Research

Every island kept its records differently, so where you start depends on where your family came from. Here is where to begin, island by island.

New to family history?

You don’t need a subscription or any experience. Start with the five steps: write down what you know, decide what you want to find out, and work back one generation at a time.

Research by island

Each guide covers the key records, the archives, what you can search online, and the surnames members are researching there. Each has a “Where should I look?” tool: tell it who you’re looking for and roughly when, and it suggests the records to try first.

  • JamaicaJamaica has some of the best-kept records in the Caribbean, from Church of England registers that go back to the 1660s to civil registration from 1878.
  • BarbadosBarbados has some of the oldest registers in the Caribbean: Church of England baptisms, marriages and burials go back to 1637 in some parishes.
  • St Kitts and NevisSt Kitts and Nevis are two islands that share one set of records.
  • Trinidad and TobagoTrinidad’s records reflect everyone who came: Spanish and French settlers, enslaved Africans, free Black American soldiers and, from 1845, indentured workers from India.
  • Other Caribbean islandsFrom Anguilla to Guyana: the records that cover every island, and the surnames members are researching.

Stuck? Ask us

Between us we cover Jamaica, the other islands and military records. Tell us who you’re looking for and what you’ve tried so far, and we’ll pass your question to the person best placed to help. Email us (cfhg.sol.birm@gmail.com). We’re all volunteers, so a reply can take a little while.

At Solihull Core Library you can use FamilySearch and Ancestry for free, and see records for some islands that aren’t online at home. Come to a meeting and we’ll show you.
