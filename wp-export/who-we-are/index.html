Who we are

Volunteers helping people trace their Caribbean families, in Solihull and online, since 2008. We love family history, and we’re here to help you find yours.

Our story so far

Some of our founder members are still on the committee today. Newer members have joined them, so between us we have a wide range of experience in researching ancestors across the Caribbean.

  1. 2008

    The group begins

    Courses in Caribbean family history are made from a ten-week British beginners’ class, reshaped for people with Caribbean heritage.

  2. 2010

    Working with WAWI

    We start working alongside the WAWI Project (Why Are West Indians in this Country). Together we host the Windrush Dinner and Dance every June.

  3. 2011

    Records at the library

    At our request, Solihull Libraries become a FamilySearch affiliate. Every Solihull library now gives free access to FamilySearch’s digitised records.

  4. 2021

    Records unlocked

    Our Barbados group finds that registers they need on FamilySearch are locked, and asks for them to be opened. They can now be viewed anywhere in the world.

  5. 2022

    Our first open day

    A Black History Month exhibition and open day at Solihull Library, on tracing ancestors from the period of slavery.

  6. 2023

    75 years of Windrush

    A free exhibition at Solihull Library through October, honouring the Windrush generation.

  7. 2025

    A visit from the Mayor

    The Mayor of Solihull visits our Black History Month exhibition and open day.

Our aim is to make Caribbean family history open to everyone.

That’s why our exhibitions and open days are free, why your first meeting is free, and why we meet on Zoom as well as in Solihull. Members now join us from across the UK, the USA, Jamaica and Trinidad.

What we do

  • Monthly meetings

    On the last Saturday of the month, at Solihull Core Library and on Zoom. Bring a name, a photograph or a question.

  • Island research groups

    Members-only groups for Jamaica, Barbados, and St Kitts and Nevis with Anguilla, working through the records together.

  • Exhibitions and open days

    Free exhibitions for Black History Month and Windrush, with open days where anyone can ask about their family.

  • Help with your research

    Tell us who you’re looking for and what you’ve tried. We’ll pass your question to the member who knows that island or record best.

Where we meet

Solihull Core Library is an Associate Family History Centre. Inside you can use almost all of FamilySearch’s online Caribbean records. At home, what you can see depends on the island: Jamaica and Barbados are almost fully open.

Who we work with

  • Solihull Libraries

    Our meetings happen at Solihull Core Library. At our request it became a FamilySearch affiliate in 2011, and every Solihull library now gives free access to FamilySearch’s digitised records.

  • The WAWI Project

    Why Are West Indians in this Country. We have worked alongside each other since around 2010 and host the Windrush Dinner and Dance together every June.

  • Family History Federation

    We are a member society of the Federation, which promotes family history research and handles our membership payments.
