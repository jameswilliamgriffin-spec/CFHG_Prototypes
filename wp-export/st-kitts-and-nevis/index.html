St Kitts and Nevis

St Kitts and Nevis are two islands that share one set of records. Early registers were lost to war, fire and hurricanes, so what survives starts later than on some islands: church baptisms from 1716, and civil registration from 1859. Our St Kitts, Nevis and Anguilla research group, for members, meets at Solihull Core Library and on Zoom.

The main record sources

  • FamilySearch

    Free. Civil registration from 1859 to 1932 and Church of England records from 1747 to 1930. You can search the indexes at home, but the images of the registers can only be viewed at a FamilySearch centre or an affiliate library.

    Search St Kitts and Nevis on FamilySearch

  • At Solihull Core Library

    Our St Kitts, Nevis and Anguilla group meets at the library because the register images can be viewed there. Solihull Libraries also list the Caribbean family history films they hold.

    See the library's list of films

  • National Archives of St Kitts and Nevis

    In Basseterre. Holds government records, registers and newspapers for both islands. Write before you visit.

  • Nevis Historical and Conservation Society

    In Charlestown. Its archive and library cover Nevis families, estates and churches.

    Visit the Nevis Historical and Conservation Society

  • Slave registers and compensation records

    Registers of enslaved people made between 1817 and 1834, at The National Archives (series T 71), and searchable on Ancestry. The Legacies of British Slavery database at UCL covers the owners and estates paid compensation.

    Search Legacies of British Slavery

  • Findmypast

    Baptisms from some St Kitts and Nevis parishes, 1716 to 1881, indexed by name. A subscription site, free to use in many libraries.

    Visit Findmypast
