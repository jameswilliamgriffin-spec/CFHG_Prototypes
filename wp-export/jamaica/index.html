Jamaica

Jamaica has some of the best-kept records in the Caribbean, from Church of England registers that go back to the 1660s to civil registration from 1878. Most of us in the group are researching Jamaican families, and our Jamaica sub-group meets regularly.

The main record sources

  • FamilySearch

    Free. Church of England registers from the 1660s and civil registration from 1878, many indexed. Image access for Jamaica has recently changed, so some images may only be viewable at a FamilySearch centre or affiliate library, like Solihull Core Library.

    Search Jamaica on FamilySearch

  • Registrar General's Department

    Jamaica's official records of births, marriages and deaths, and the older church registers. Order certificates here.

    Visit the Registrar General's Department

  • Jamaica Archives, Spanish Town

    Wills, deeds, land records, the island's older registers and much more. Plan ahead: check opening before you go.

    Find out about the Jamaica Archives

  • Slave registers and compensation records

    Registers of enslaved people made every three years from 1817 to 1834, at The National Archives (series T 71), and searchable on Ancestry. The Legacies of British Slavery database at UCL covers the owners and estates paid compensation.

    Search Legacies of British Slavery

  • Jamaican Family Search

    Transcribed almanacs, lists of landholders and other early sources, gathered over many years.

    Visit Jamaican Family Search

  • At Solihull Core Library

    Solihull Libraries list the Caribbean family history films they hold, and you can use FamilySearch there at affiliate level. Ask us at a meeting and we'll show you.

    See the library's list of films
